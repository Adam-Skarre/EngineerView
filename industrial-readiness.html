Industrial Readiness Field Guide

Before the digital factory.

The path to adaptive operations begins with stable work, trustworthy data, connected systems, explicit decision rights, and the people who operate them.

Executive finding

Digitization cannot outrun operating discipline.

Industrial technology programs often begin with a platform selection. Engineer View starts one level lower: Is the process stable enough to represent? Is the data trustworthy enough to decide from? Are system boundaries and human authority clear? If not, more software can scale ambiguity.

Key takeaways
  1. Observability requires definitions and ownership before dashboards.
  2. Integration is valuable when it shortens a decision loop, not when it maximizes data movement.
  3. Adaptive operation requires governed authority and trained people—not autonomous theater.

01 · Readiness model

Four states of operating maturity.

01

Instrumented

Critical conditions are measured with known context and calibration.

Question: Can we see the state?
02

Observable

Signals can be interpreted across process, asset, quality, and time.

Question: Can we explain it?
03

Governed

Definitions, access, decisions, and exceptions have accountable owners.

Question: Can we trust and act?
04

Adaptive

Learning changes the process through bounded, monitored authority.

Question: Can the system improve safely?

Engineer View framework. This is a qualitative decision model, not an industry benchmark or certification score.

02 · Readiness diagnostic

Five domains determine the ceiling.

DomainEvidence of readinessWarning signal
Process stabilityStandard work and known exception pathsTechnology automates unresolved variation
Data integrityDefinitions, lineage, timing, and ownershipTeams debate the meaning of the metric
System integrationInterfaces aligned to an operating decisionData moves without reducing cycle time
Decision rightsAuthority, escalation, and evidence thresholdsAlerts exist but nobody owns the action
Workforce capabilityOperators understand, challenge, and improve toolsKnowledge sits only with integrators

03 · Implementation sequence

Start with one consequential loop.

1

Name the decision.

Select an operating decision with a measurable outcome and clear owner.

2

Trace the evidence.

Map signals, definitions, latency, quality, and failure modes.

3

Reduce the loop.

Integrate only what shortens detection, interpretation, action, or learning.

4

Bound the authority.

Define what the system may recommend, execute, and escalate.

5

Scale the pattern.

Reuse proven standards across adjacent assets and processes.

The objective is not a digital factory. It is a factory that can observe, decide, and improve.
