Executive view

The headline is not the system.

A market label compresses many different structures into one phrase. “Private credit,” “AI demand,” and “high valuations” become useful only after the underlying mechanism is made visible. The engineering task is to identify the load path, the bottleneck, and the evidence that would change the decision.

Three conclusions
  1. Credit risk depends on borrower capacity, loan structure, and the liquidity promised to investors—not the category name alone.
  2. AI infrastructure is constrained by deliverable power, interconnection, equipment, and construction timelines—not announced demand alone.
  3. A high valuation increases sensitivity to disappointment; it does not establish the timing or direction of a market reversal.

01 · Private credit

Risk lives in the structure.

The Federal Reserve estimates that private-credit loans reached about $1.4 trillion in the second half of 2025—large enough to matter, but too varied for one conclusion. The relevant variables are borrower cash generation, debt-service capacity, collateral, seniority, documentation, sector concentration, and the liquidity offered to investors.

Traditional private-debt funds commonly lock investor capital for years. Newer semi-liquid vehicles permit periodic redemptions subject to limits. Redemption requests increased in late 2025 and early 2026, yet the Federal Reserve also reported that markets continued to function normally. The disciplined view holds both facts at once: liquidity pressure rose, but that evidence did not establish system-wide failure.

Engineer View test

Trace the funding path before judging the asset class.

  • Who bears first loss?
  • What cash flow services the debt?
  • Which liquidity promise can be called first?
  • What happens when refinancing arrives later or costs more?

02 · AI infrastructure

Compute can scale faster than power systems.

The EIA reports that U.S. electricity demand grew about 1.7% annually between 2020 and 2025 after growing only 0.1% annually from 2005 through 2019, with data centers among the principal drivers. But aggregate megawatts do not identify a project’s critical path.

The binding constraint may be generation, transmission, an interconnection study, a substation, long-lead equipment, cooling, construction labor, or a customer commitment. Investment analysis should therefore distinguish requested capacity, contracted capacity, deliverable power, energized capacity, and economically utilized capacity.

Demand is a signal. Deliverable capacity is the system.

03 · Public markets

Price and earnings answer different questions.

The Federal Reserve’s May 2026 review found the forward price-to-earnings ratio of S&P 500 companies near the upper end of its historical distribution. Separately, the Bureau of Economic Analysis estimated first-quarter 2026 corporate profits from current production at an annual rate of $4,426.5 billion.

Those observations can coexist. Aggregate profits do not show that every security is fairly priced, while a demanding valuation does not prove that current income is fictitious or that reversal is imminent. The useful test is company-level: revenue concentration, margins, reinvestment, cash conversion, balance-sheet obligations, and the growth already embedded in price.

Engineer View test

Which operating assumption must hold for the current price to make sense?

  • Growth duration
  • Margin durability
  • Capital intensity
  • Cash conversion
